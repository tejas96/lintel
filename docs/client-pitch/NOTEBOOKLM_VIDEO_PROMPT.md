# NotebookLM video — how to make it

## Steps

1. Open NotebookLM and create a new notebook.
2. Add 2 sources:
   - The proposal deck as a PDF. Export it from the deck first.
   - The story file `studio-story-for-notebooklm.md` from this folder. You can upload it as a file,
     or paste its text as a "copied text" source.
3. Choose **Video Overview**, and open its customise option (the pencil or "Customize" button).
4. If NotebookLM asks for a format, choose the longer explainer format, not the short brief one.
   If it offers visual styles, choose a clean, calm, minimal one.
5. Set the language to English, or to the language your client prefers.
6. Paste the prompt below into the box that asks what the video should focus on.
7. Generate the video. Watch it once before you send it, and check that it follows the rules in the prompt.

NotebookLM's buttons and options change over time. If one of the names above is different, use
the closest one.

## The prompt — copy everything between the lines

---

Make a warm, simple, confident explainer video for the owner of an interior design and
architecture studio in India. The owner is busy and not technical. After watching, the owner must
feel "they understand my studio" and want to start this project.

Tell it as ONE journey, in this order, and keep each part short:

1. A normal day for the owner today. Use the timeline from the sources (calls at 09:10, the third
   reminder at 10:30, a task that never started, leave on WhatsApp, a vendor delivery that did not
   arrive, a client asking for an update, planning everyone's work at 20:00). End this part with:
   "Today, you are the system."
2. What this quietly costs: the owner's time, late surprises, team energy, client trust. Say clearly
   that it is nobody's fault.
3. The real problem: seven parts of the studio that don't talk to each other — priorities,
   ownership, project flow, visibility, leave, vendors and sellers, rewards.
4. Our approach in one line: "Make the right action the easiest action", and the four principles.
5. Follow ONE example project, the "Mehta Residence", through the nine steps: a project that plans
   itself from a template; a clear daily plan for each designer, with reasons; clear ownership and a
   calm follow-up ladder; the owner's 30-second morning view, with the at-risk example (impact, next
   step, who acts, alert at 4 PM); client changes with impact, client confirmation and history;
   leave with impact checks, handovers and emergencies; vendors and sellers (vendor list, compare
   quotes, purchase order, track delivery, receive on site with photos, pay on time, vendor
   scorecard, material approvals, honest vendor delays); site work from a phone in seconds; fair
   rewards (the ₹2,000 milestone example, what counts, transparency, the right to challenge, no
   automatic deductions, team rewards).
6. Everyone sees what they need — owner, project manager, designer, site supervisor — and private
   things stay private.
7. Smart help in three layers (clear rules now, early warnings as history grows, a plain-language
   assistant later). Stress that the system never approves leave, changes pay, moves deadlines or
   reassigns work on its own: people decide.
8. Before and after: end this part on "from chasing everyone to deciding what matters".
9. First, we agree ten simple rules together in a workshop.
10. Five build steps, each one ready to try, so there is no big surprise at the end.
11. The next step: one two-hour workshop. Close with: "A studio that runs well — even on the days
    you are not watching."

Rules:

- Speak in short, simple sentences and everyday words. Explain any idea with a studio example
  (layouts, 3D renders, client approvals, site visits, vendor deliveries).
- Spend the most time on the owner's pain, the Mehta Residence journey and the vendor part. Move
  quickly through the rest.
- Be respectful of the team. Never suggest employees are lazy; the problem is the system, not the
  people.
- Present this as a proposal and a plan. Do NOT say the software already exists, do NOT describe
  any real app screens, and do NOT mention programming languages, frameworks, databases, cloud
  providers or other technical details.
- All numbers are examples (87 out of 100, ₹2,000, 2 working days, the times of day). Say that they
  are examples. Do NOT invent any statistics, percentages, savings, prices, dates or client names
  that are not in the sources.
- Do not quote prices or a timeline. Say that the plan and dates are agreed together after the
  workshop.
- Use Indian context naturally (₹, WhatsApp, site visits) and keep the tone premium and calm, the
  way a good design studio presents to its own clients.
- On screen, prefer simple visuals: a day timeline, a seven-part circle, a nine-step journey line,
  a calm escalation ladder, a vendor flow, and a before-and-after table. Keep text on screen to a
  few words.
- Aim for about 6 to 8 minutes.

## A second, shorter version (optional)

For a 2-minute teaser to send before the meeting, use the same sources and this prompt:

---

Make a 2-minute teaser for the owner of an interior design and architecture studio. Show only
three things: (1) the owner's day today, ending with "Today, you are the system"; (2) the idea "make
the right action the easiest action" with the seven connected parts, including vendors and sellers;
(3) the promise: "A studio that runs well — even on the days you are not watching", and an
invitation to a two-hour workshop. Use simple words. Do not say the software exists, do not show app
screens, do not mention technology, and do not invent numbers, prices or dates.
